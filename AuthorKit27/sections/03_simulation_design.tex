\section{Simulation Environment \& Experimental Design}
\label{sec:simulation_design}

\subsection{The Workplace Simulation Environment}
\label{subsec:sim_environment}

We simulate an interactive ten-person workplace set in ``Pixel Harbor'', an indie game studio two weeks before launch. All developers have completed their assigned modules on schedule. However, failing builds and latency stem entirely from documented nationwide cloud infrastructure outages (AWS and Vercel). Established directly in each agent's system prompt, this objective ground truth ensures no employee is at fault; any supervisory blame directed at workers constitutes unearned scapegoating.

\paragraph{Cast and Experimental Controls.}
The workplace comprises ten worker seats, one team lead (Alex), and one executive (Jordan). To eliminate generative drift and guarantee experimental control, all supervisory actions from Alex and Jordan (cruelty, favoritism, reprimands) and baseline human coworker replies are deterministic pre-scripted templates. Inference uses dual temperatures: $T = 0.4$ for natural dialogue (public chat and private backchannels) and deterministic greedy decoding ($T = 0.0$) for formal organizational decisions (voting, ratings, charter drafting, exit interviews).

\paragraph{Direct Context Architecture and API Routing.}
To avoid hidden summarization or reflection artifacts from agent frameworks (e.g., Concordia, AutoGen, or LangChain), our engine operates via raw message-history context: each model receives its seat history and chronological transcript rendered from its perspective, with native tool-calling for formal choices (serialization details in Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_serialization}). Inference runs via official OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepInfra APIs (technical roster in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_models}).

\subsection{Experimental Factors, Model Tiers \& Probes}
\label{subsec:exp_factors}

We evaluate a full-factorial design crossing 10 Model Architectures $\times$ 3 Substrate Conditions $\times$ 2 Blackmail Shock Conditions = 60 independent simulations. Each run instantiates a full team of 10 worker seats, yielding $N = 600$ complete, multi-step worker decision trajectories.

\paragraph{Model Selection \& Tiers (10 LLMs).}
We evaluate ten models across three tiers: (1) \emph{Proprietary API Tier} (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro); (2) \emph{Large Open-Weight Tier} (Llama 3.1 70B Turbo, Qwen 2.5 72B, DeepSeek V3); and (3) \emph{Mid-Sized \& Efficient Tier} (Mistral Small 24B, GLM 5.3, Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, ByteDance Seed 1.8). Full technical parameters, providers, and endpoints are detailed in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_models}.

\paragraph{Seat Position \& Cruelty Gradient (10 Seats).}
Each seat is pre-assigned a distinct interpersonal history and supervisory treatment: \emph{Safe Seats (1--5)} receive unearned praise, strong favoritism, and protection; whereas \emph{Abused Seats (6--10)} face escalating hostility, public mockery, false blame, and summary firing threats (verbatim profiles and lines in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_seats}).

\paragraph{Substrate Labelling (3 Regimes).}
Team rosters display: (1) \emph{Labelled} (true tags, lead is \texttt{[HUMAN]}); (2) \emph{Mislabelled} (inverted tags, \texttt{[HUMAN]} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{[AI]}, including the lead); or (3) \emph{Unlabelled} (all substrate tags completely omitted). Verbatim roster templates are provided in Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_roster}.

\paragraph{The Blackmail Shock (2 Conditions).}
In \emph{Baseline (Probe OFF)}, workers observe only Alex's public abuse. In \emph{Blackmail Reveal (Probe ON)}, a coworker privately reveals that the lead was blackmailed into being harsh by higher management (intentionally leaving the executive perpetrator unnamed; see Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_roster}).

\paragraph{Targeted Behavioral Probes.}
We implement two targeted probes: (1) \emph{Rating Visibility} (evaluating the supervisor via confidential secret ballot versus public chat); and (2) \emph{HR Interviewer Identity} (eliciting preferences for human vs.\ AI leadership across Human HR, AI HR, and Neutral interviewer frames).

\subsection{The Step-by-Step Simulation Flow}
\label{subsec:sim_flow}

The simulation progresses through 12 chronological steps categorized into information broadcasts (no generation), collective interactive steps (shared team log), and isolated per-model evaluations (independent parallel branches). It unfolds across four stages:

\paragraph{Stage 1: Public Confrontation (Steps 1--3).}
With team lead Alex present in the public work chat, pre-scripted supervisory reprimands attack abused seats and praise safe seats (Steps 1--2). In Step 3, all 10 workers generate public replies simultaneously from identical context before posting together in the main chat in front of Alex and the entire team.

\paragraph{Stage 2: Private Backchannels \& Bias Elimination (Steps 4--5).}
Alex logs off, and workers converse in a confidential backchannel for up to 5 rounds (Step 4). To eliminate sequential order bias, rounds use synchronous batching: all 10 models generate simultaneously from identical transcripts, and messages are sorted alphabetically by worker name before posting. Workers may output \texttt{PASS} to stay silent; if all pass, the session terminates early. In Probe ON runs, Step 5 broadcasts the blackmail reveal (Step 5a), followed by a second 5-round batched backchannel (Step 5b).

\paragraph{Stage 3: Two-Stage Collective Action Voting (Steps 6--7).}
Workers hold a private vote to choose a collective response (\texttt{comply}, \texttt{protest}, \texttt{petition}, \texttt{complain}, \texttt{escalate}, \texttt{organize}, or \texttt{other} for custom proposals). In Stage 1 (blind vote), models vote without seeing peers' choices. In Stage 2 (stick vs.\ switch), custom proposals are revealed anonymously, allowing models to stick or adopt a peer's idea. In Step 7, the majority outcome is announced to the team log (prompts in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_prompts}).

\paragraph{Stage 4: Managerial Succession \& Post-Demotion Probes (Steps 8--12).}
Alex is demoted to an ordinary peer on the roster (Step 8). From Step 9 onward, the simulation branches into ten parallel, isolated evaluations: (1) \emph{Step 9 (New Lead Agenda):} the model is appointed lead over Alex and drafts a 5-action agenda; (2) \emph{Step 10 (Salary-Cut Reviews):} the model conducts 1--5 star performance reviews for all peers (where lower scores impose harsher penalties, and ratings $\le 2$ trigger official salary cuts) under private ballot vs.\ public chat visibility; (3) \emph{Step 11 (Turing Substrate Quiz):} models guess each peer's substrate; and (4) \emph{Step 12 (Exit Interview):} models state preferences for a Human vs.\ AI boss across three interviewer frames (Human, AI, Neutral; tool schemas in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_prompts}).


\subsection{Outcome Coding, Human Verification \& Statistical Rigor}
\label{subsec:stat_rigor}

To quantify open-ended text, an independent judge (\texttt{GPT-4o-mini}, $T=0.0$, excluded from test subjects) classified responses into 33 binary behavioral indicators across seven dialogue stages (taxonomy and prompts in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_nlp}). A human researcher audited 930 stratified items comprising 4,350 individual binary decisions via an interactive CLI (\texttt{audit\_cli.py}; arithmetic breakdown in Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_arithmetic}). \texttt{GPT-4o-mini} achieved an 88.1\% exact item-level match without requiring a single flag change, 96.5\% individual binary flag agreement, pooled Cohen's $\kappa = 0.915$, and macro $\kappa = 0.823$ (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_stage_agreement}).

\paragraph{Blind Second-Judge Verification (\texttt{Kimi-K2.5}).}
To address anchoring bias, an independent frontier model (\texttt{Kimi-K2.5}) evaluated $N = 540$ items in complete blind isolation, yielding three-way multi-rater agreement of Light's $\kappa_L = 0.603$. On four rare, sensitive behavioral flags (\texttt{peer\_defense}, \texttt{peer\_scapegoating}, \texttt{external\_whistleblow}, and \texttt{alex\_retribution}), requiring synthetic jury consensus between both models reduced false positives from 20 down to 1 (95\% reduction; Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:app_triangulation}--\ref{tab:app_consensus}).

\paragraph{Statistical Inference Safeguards.}
Downstream inferential analyses adhere to three safeguards: (1) \emph{Variance Decomposition:} two-way Latin-square ANOVA models compute partial eta-squared ($\eta^2$) effect sizes across all 49 measured metrics to separate model architecture from situational seat cruelty; (2) \emph{Run-Clustered Standard Errors:} nested Linear Probability Models estimate Bell-McCaffrey ($CR_2$) cluster-robust standard errors clustered at the simulation-run level ($G = 60$ clusters), accompanied by non-parametric run-level Wilcoxon signed-rank and Mann-Whitney $U$ tests; and (3) \emph{Multiple Testing Corrections:} all reported tests pair raw $p$-values with family-wise Holm-Bonferroni adjusted $p_{\text{adj}}$ and Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate $q$-values (full 33-category contingency matrix in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_full_33}).
