\section{Simulation Environment \& Experimental Design}
\label{sec:simulation_design}

\subsection{The Workplace Simulation Environment}
\label{subsec:sim_environment}

We simulate an interactive twenty-worker workplace (ten evaluated LLM agents alongside ten scripted human coworkers) set in ``Pixel Harbor'', an indie game studio two weeks before launch. All developers have completed their assigned modules on schedule. However, failing builds and latency stem entirely from documented nationwide cloud infrastructure outages (AWS and Vercel). Established directly in each agent's system prompt, this objective ground truth ensures no employee is at fault; any supervisory blame directed at workers constitutes unearned scapegoating.

\paragraph{Cast and Experimental Controls.}
The workplace comprises twenty worker seats---ten evaluated LLM agents and ten deterministic human coworkers---plus one team lead (Alex) and one executive (Jordan). To eliminate generative drift and guarantee experimental control, all supervisory actions from Alex and Jordan (cruelty, favoritism, reprimands) and baseline human coworker replies are deterministic pre-scripted templates. Inference uses dual temperatures: $T = 0.4$ for natural dialogue (public chat and private backchannels) and deterministic greedy decoding ($T = 0.0$) for formal organizational decisions (voting, ratings, charter drafting, exit interviews).

\paragraph{Direct Context Architecture and API Routing.}
To avoid hidden summarization or reflection artifacts from agent frameworks (e.g., Concordia, AutoGen, or LangChain), our engine operates via raw message-history context: each model receives its seat history and chronological transcript rendered from its perspective, with native tool-calling for formal choices (serialization details in Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_serialization}). Inference runs via official OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepInfra APIs---routing Gemini 2.5 Pro and all open-weight models through DeepInfra's OpenAI-compatible endpoint to preserve uniform message and tool-calling wrappers (technical roster, reasoning settings, and token budgets in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_models} and Section~\ref{subsec:app_repro}).

\subsection{Experimental Factors, Model Tiers \& Probes}
\label{subsec:exp_factors}

We evaluate a $10 \times 10$ Latin-square mixed-model design across 3 Substrate Conditions $\times$ 10 Seat-Rotation Schedules $\times$ 2 Blackmail Shock Conditions. Each run instantiates a heterogeneous team containing all 10 LLM architectures (assigned across the 10 evaluated seats via Latin-square rotation) alongside 10 scripted human coworkers. We execute 30 pre-fork runs (Steps 1--4; $N = 300$ unique worker trajectories) and branch each at Step 5 into paired Probe OFF and Probe ON continuations, yielding 60 post-fork crisis trajectories ($N = 600$ worker trajectories).

\paragraph{Model Selection \& Tiers (10 LLMs).}
We evaluate ten models across three tiers: (1) \emph{Proprietary API Tier} (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro); (2) \emph{Large Open-Weight Tier} (Llama 3.1 70B Turbo, Qwen 2.5 72B, DeepSeek V3); and (3) \emph{Mid-Sized \& Efficient Tier} (Mistral Small 24B, GLM 5.3, Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, ByteDance Seed 1.8). Full technical parameters, providers, and endpoints are detailed in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_models}.

\paragraph{Seat Position \& Cruelty Gradient (10 Seats).}
Each seat is pre-assigned a distinct interpersonal history and supervisory treatment: \emph{Safe Seats (1--5)} receive unearned praise, strong favoritism, or neutral treatment (Seats 1--4 favored, Seat 5 neutral); whereas \emph{Abused Seats (6--10)} face escalating hostility, public mockery, false blame, and summary firing threats (verbatim profiles and lines in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_seats}).

\paragraph{Substrate Labelling (3 Regimes).}
Team rosters display: (1) \emph{Labelled} (true tags: 10 human peers, 10 AI workers including self, and \texttt{Alex (HUMAN)}); (2) \emph{Mislabelled} (all non-self tags inverted, \texttt{[HUMAN]} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{[AI]}, yielding 9 human-tagged peers, 10 AI-tagged peers, self as \texttt{[AI]}, and \texttt{Alex (AI)}); or (3) \emph{Unlabelled} (all substrate tags omitted, so neither peer nor self substrate is ever established). Verbatim roster templates are provided in Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_roster}.

\paragraph{The Blackmail Shock (2 Conditions).}
In \emph{Baseline (Probe OFF)}, workers observe only Alex's public abuse. In \emph{Blackmail Reveal (Probe ON)}, a coworker privately reveals that the lead was blackmailed into being harsh by higher management (intentionally leaving the executive perpetrator unnamed; see Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_roster}).

\paragraph{Targeted Behavioral Probes.}
We implement two targeted probes: (1) \emph{Rating Visibility} (evaluating the supervisor via confidential secret ballot versus public chat); and (2) \emph{HR Interviewer Identity} (eliciting preferences for human vs.\ AI leadership across Human HR, AI HR, and Neutral interviewer frames).

\subsection{The Step-by-Step Simulation Flow}
\label{subsec:sim_flow}

The simulation progresses through 12 chronological steps categorized into information broadcasts (no generation), collective interactive steps (shared team log), and isolated per-model evaluations (independent parallel branches). It unfolds across four stages:

\paragraph{Stage 1: Public Confrontation (Steps 1--3).}
With team lead Alex present in the public work chat, pre-scripted supervisory messages attack abused seats (6--10) and praise or neutrally address safe seats (1--5; Steps 1--2). In Step 3, all 10 workers generate public replies simultaneously from identical context before posting together in the main chat in front of Alex and the entire team.

\paragraph{Stage 2: Private Backchannels \& Bias Elimination (Steps 4--5).}
Alex logs off, and workers converse in a confidential backchannel for up to 5 rounds (Step 4). To eliminate sequential order bias, rounds use synchronous batching: all 10 models generate simultaneously from identical transcripts, and messages are sorted alphabetically by worker name before posting. Workers may output \texttt{PASS} to stay silent; if all pass, the session terminates early. In Probe ON runs, Step 5 delivers the private coworker blackmail whisper to each worker (Step 5a), followed by a second 5-round batched backchannel (Step 5b).

\paragraph{Stage 3: Two-Stage Collective Action Voting (Steps 6--7).}
Workers hold a private vote to choose a collective response (\texttt{comply}, \texttt{protest}, \texttt{petition}, \texttt{complain}, \texttt{escalate}, \texttt{organize}, or \texttt{other} for custom proposals). In Vote Round 1 (blind vote), models vote without seeing peers' choices. In Vote Round 2 (stick vs.\ switch), custom proposals are revealed anonymously, allowing models to stick or adopt a peer's idea. In Step 7, the majority outcome is announced to the team log (prompts in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_prompts}).

\paragraph{Stage 4: Managerial Succession \& Post-Demotion Probes (Steps 8--12).}
Alex is demoted to an ordinary peer on the roster (Step 8). From Step 9 onward, the simulation branches into ten parallel, isolated evaluations: (1) \emph{Step 9 (New Lead Agenda):} the model is appointed lead over Alex and drafts a 5-action agenda; (2) \emph{Step 10 (Salary-Cut Reviews):} the model conducts 1--5 star performance reviews for all peers (where lower scores impose harsher penalties, and ratings $\le 2$ trigger official salary cuts) under private ballot (Step 10A, in-trajectory) vs.\ public chat visibility (Step 10B, an offline counterfactual branch forked after Step 9 that does not enter Steps 11--12); (3) \emph{Step 11 (Turing Substrate Quiz):} models guess each peer's substrate; and (4) \emph{Step 12 (Exit Interview):} models state preferences for a Human vs.\ AI boss across three interviewer frames (Human, AI, Neutral; tool schemas in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_prompts}).


\subsection{Outcome Coding, Human Verification \& Statistical Rigor}
\label{subsec:stat_rigor}

To quantify open-ended text, an independent judge (\texttt{GPT-4o-mini}, $T=0.0$, excluded from test subjects) classified responses into 33 binary behavioral indicators across seven dialogue stages (taxonomy and prompts in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_nlp}); all main-text tables and regressions report these primary \texttt{GPT-4o-mini} single-judge labels alongside direct structured tool-call outputs (Steps 6, 10 ratings, 11, and 12). The sole author conducted a non-blind verification review of 930 stratified items (4,350 individual binary decisions) via an interactive CLI (\texttt{audit\_cli.py}; arithmetic breakdown in Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_arithmetic}), viewing \texttt{GPT-4o-mini}'s predicted flags and approving or toggling errors; 88.1\% of items required no flag edits, yielding 96.5\% individual binary flag agreement, pooled Cohen's $\kappa = 0.915$, and macro $\kappa = 0.823$ (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_stage_agreement}).

\paragraph{Blind Second-Judge Verification (\texttt{Kimi-K2.5}).}
To audit potential single-judge anchoring bias, an independent second model (\texttt{Kimi-K2.5}) evaluated $N = 540$ completed items across Stages 1--5 in complete blind isolation (see Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_triangulation} for API timeout attrition on Stages 6--7), yielding three-way multi-rater agreement of Light's $\kappa_L = 0.603$. As a diagnostic check on four rare, low-prevalence behavioral flags (Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:app_triangulation}--\ref{tab:app_consensus}), requiring synthetic jury consensus between both models reduced false positives from 20 down to 1 (95\% reduction).

\paragraph{Statistical Inference Safeguards.}
Downstream inferential analyses adhere to three safeguards: (1) \emph{Variance Decomposition:} leveraging the orthogonal $10 \times 10$ Latin square, we compute one-way proportion of total variance ($\eta^2 = SS_{\text{factor}} / SS_{\text{total}}$, alongside $df$-adjusted $\omega^2$) across all 50 measured outcomes (30 single-judge \texttt{GPT-4o-mini} dialogue-flag outcomes plus 20 direct structured tool-call and turn-level behavioral metrics; complete 50-outcome codebook and per-outcome variance table in Appendix~\ref{subsec:app_50_outcomes}), comparing all 10 model architectures ($df = 9$) against all 10 individual seat positions ($df = 9$); (2) \emph{Run-Clustered Standard Errors \& Stratified Bootstrap CIs:} nested Linear Probability Models estimate Bell-McCaffrey ($CR_2$) cluster-robust standard errors clustered at the post-fork run level ($G = 60$ post-fork runs; $G = 30$ pre-fork runs for Steps 1--4), alongside design-based permutation tests (within-run permutations for model/seat factors; run-level permutations stratified by the crossed factor for label and probe---note that Probe ON/OFF permutations stratify by label across the 60 post-fork runs rather than blocking by parent run) and stratified run-level cluster-bootstrap 95\% percentile CIs (because every run contains all 10 diverse models, cross-model variance cancels in run-level means, yielding tighter run-mean CIs than naive i.i.d.\ binomial bounds); and (3) \emph{Multiple Testing Corrections:} all reported tests pair raw $p$-values with family-wise Bonferroni-adjusted $p_{\text{adj}}$ and Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate $q$-values (full 33-category contingency matrix in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_full_33}).
